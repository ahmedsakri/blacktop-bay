## Release 1 — dependable play, controls and saves

- **Lobby and dialogs:** race-panel rows retain their natural height, short desktop screens use a horizontal circuit card, and constrained panels scroll without shrinking the circuit card underneath another control. Long names and goals wrap; primary actions retain at least 44 CSS px targets. Portrait scrolling content has separate space above fixed navigation. Nested dialog Back restores the parent launcher, including a rebuilt button inside a collapsed disclosure; final close returns to the original available opener. Keyboard trapping includes disclosure summaries.
- **Race timing:** rendered frames and simulation time are separated. Ordinary elapsed time is fed to the existing 120 Hz physics accumulator in chunks no larger than 1/30 second. Tests compare 30/60/120 Hz schedules and repeated 100–250 ms stalls. An active gap longer than 300 ms contributes no simulation time and requests a visible pause. Countdown overflow is carried into the first racing step. Hidden tabs, graphics interruption and explicit pause do not add driving time; returning requires the appropriate player action.
- **Graphics recovery:** context loss clears held input, holds the run, stops active audio demand and shows recovery status. World/garage reflection resources, shadows and postprocessing are rebuilt, then programs compiled. Generation checks reject obsolete attempts after each asynchronous boundary; another loss invalidates an in-flight restore. Car selection keeps the previous model if graphics preparation is interrupted. The recovery UI offers retry, backup and reload if restoration fails. Successful recovery returns to the relevant view; an interrupted race remains paused.
- **Records:** a personal best is called saved only after the write verifies by exact read-back. Failed or unverifiable writes retain a scoped session candidate; changing views or completing a slower run cannot erase it. Results show a session-only message and Retry save / Export unsaved records. Exported record files can be validated and merged through Save backup, preserving faster existing times and unrelated progress. Full portable backups also include pending session records. Authentication data and analytics consent remain excluded.
- **Tour continuation:** the career and next-round preferences are staged together behind a durable journal, verified before the visible selection changes, and rolled back on failure. Once a journal is known, denied storage keeps recovery blocking until the saved state can be established. Actual finish times stay intact; leaving a completed round classifies unfinished rivals as DNF with zero points. No opponent finish time is invented.
- **Driving:** manual reset uses legal-road/obstacle candidates behind validated progress, checks visible-body clearance and vertical traffic separation, and does nothing if no safe candidate exists. It neither awards progress nor refills Nitro. In toggle mode the first tap starts Nitro, a tap in a valid Burst/Perfect window upgrades and keeps the boost active, and a tap outside that window or after the upgrade stops it. Physical press identifiers prevent a held input or brake release from becoming a second tap. Help, settings and school instructions reflect this behavior.

Implementation details: [UI evidence](ui-reliability-2026-10-02.md), [driving/audio/record contract](../docs/driving-audio-records.md). The existing 18-event campaign, car mastery, per-circuit setups, original asset attribution and privacy choices are preserved.

These are asset counts, configuration bounds and accounting estimates. A primitive count is not a measured full-scene draw-call count, and an estimated cache total is not measured driver/GPU memory.

Three heavy low-detail sources have optional self-hosted KTX2 variants, selected only when the device supports a suitable GPU compression format. Failure or lack of support falls back to the original WebP source. Geometry, UVs, authored material roles and credits are retained.

| Car | Original GLB → KTX2 GLB bytes | RGBA+mip → conservative 8bpp+mip texture estimate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| McLaren P1 GTR | 1,429,772 → 4,220,392 | 68 → 17 MiB |
| Porsche 930 Turbo | 2,346,336 → 4,843,888 | 30 → 7.50 MiB |
| Lamborghini Gallardo | 894,272 → 1,754,232 | 24.33 → 6.08 MiB |

This trial trades larger delivery files for approximately 75% smaller texture block storage at the stated 8bpp target. Driver overhead and block rounding are unmeasured. It does not claim faster downloads or catalogue-wide GPU compression.

Automatic quality now includes repeated severe intervals above 150 ms instead of discarding them. At least four consecutive samples and two seconds of bounded pressure trigger a reduction, with a four-second cooldown; each sample contributes at most 500 ms. Severe intervals also enter ordinary p75 windows, so alternating stalls remain visible. A single hitch does not force a downgrade. Intentional battery-saver cadence, menus, hidden tabs and loading are excluded by the caller. Existing mobile sharpness profiles and the 1× automatic floor remain. Mobile render scheduling remains 30 Hz for menu/garage, 15 Hz paused, and at most 60 Hz racing, or 30 Hz racing with Battery saver. These scheduling limits do not establish delivered FPS or reduced temperature.

## Release 3 — coherent presentation and sound

Harbor Flow, Fuji Skyline and San Francisco Hills coordinate sky tint, fog, sun/fill colour and reflection intensity. A single reflection rebuild after the panorama becomes ready captures the graded sky. Harbor sails gain tension edges; Fuji roofs gain ridge/eave trim and braces; San Francisco shelters gain fascia, braces and twelve-mark clocks. Geometry stays in existing spatial/material batches. The three viewing terraces retain 24 low-detail or 42 high-detail spectators total, with coordinated regional clothing and one filming observer per terrace.

Six factory body-paint profiles distinguish the Aventador, Ferrari 458, McLaren P1 GTR, Porsche 911 GT3, Nissan GT-R and BMW i8. Only identified physical paint materials receive the clearcoat profile. Authored texture maps, metal/roughness response, glass, carbon, badges and livery remain; custom paint/finish choices still override it. Distance cars retain their cheaper standard materials. UI supporting labels, settings focus and record recovery actions use the shared palette and illustrated icons.

Rival audio now follows the actual driver-right direction and three-dimensional attenuation/radial motion. Crowd ambience uses world-space spectator zones, including venue height, with a 95 m default radius and maximum blending across overlapping zones. The mix retains three nearby rival voices and four impact voices. Exhaust/induction layers use the same smoothed engine load, rev and shift state; a Nitro mode change restarts its onset even when toggle input stays active. An idle/hidden/muted audio graph fades before a 240 ms suspension and resumes only through the existing authorized audio lifecycle; a rejected resume waits for another gesture. No new samples, recordings or network audio assets were added. These are authored procedural sounds, not manufacturer engine recordings or measured acoustic realism.
