# Recorded engine layer — all 33 cars

Every catalogue car blends a real licensed recording with authored synthesis: **33 cars, 22 active banks, 26 bundled banks, 4,234,024 bytes total**. Seven studio sources, one CC0 Elise source and CC BY 250 GTO and Gallardo sources now improve fourteen car assignments. Only the selected bank downloads after audio unlock; the new Gallardo idle bank is **60,044 bytes**, the preceding nine additions are **180,044 bytes** each, and the unchanged largest bank is **199,244 bytes**. Four legacy banks remain unassigned for reproduction of earlier mixes.

There are **six identified base-model matches**: 570S Coupé, Aventador, Testarossa, Elise, 250 GTO and Gallardo. Source or target metadata does not specify the complete matching year/trim/build, so this is **not 100% exact specification coverage**. The 458 source does not certify Spider; AMG GT uses GT R; GT-R 2018 uses a modified 2012 R35; R8 LMS uses a road R8. Other McLarens now use a related 570S recording. Rimacs still use Tesla, and Veyron/i8/R18/919 retain explicit engine-layout mismatches. [The complete 33-car inventory](../public/assets/audio/ENGINE-COVERAGE.json) and [checked source decisions](../public/assets/audio/ENGINE-CANDIDATES.json) make these limits machine-readable.

The approved softer mix is unchanged: no changes to `audio.js`, `driving-sound.js`, `recorded-engine.js` or per-car pitch/tone/gain settings in this source upgrade. Liquid Lines remains the sole lobby soundtrack.

## Processing and mix

`scripts/prepare-engine-recordings.py --sources <directory> --output <directory>` reproduces all 26 WAV banks with FFmpeg and Python’s standard library. The manifest is the sole interval specification. Before any output is written, every selected source must match its reviewed SHA-256; extra diesel takes are checked too. `--only` restricts work to named banks and `--manifest` permits a reviewed alternate manifest. Downloads remain a deliberate separate provenance check. Input is the public HQ MP3 preview for Freesound banks, original Aston WAV and Honda/250 GTO Ogg files, original 96 kHz/24-bit Sonniss studio WAVs, and the public Flickr MP4 transcode for Gallardo. Raw source takes and source-page HTML are not in the production bundle.

Processing uses mono 24 kHz PCM16, 65 Hz high-pass and 5.8 kHz low-pass filtering, DC removal, cosine overlap, exact endpoint correction and RMS/peak normalization. Each source interval is checked against decoded length. Every bank remains below 256 KiB; loop peaks are at most 0.66. We exclude startup/door transients and silence separators from the selected driving loops, and avoid the Murciélago take’s final loud rev. Source compression and background/road texture are not magically removed; these are compact arcade layers, not studio-isolated stems.

Up to three looping AudioBufferSource voices crossfade neighboring authored rev bands at equal power. Pitch stays within 0.90–1.20. Per-car pitch/tone/gain values differentiate related models without inventing measured RPM. Load gain, shift torque and filtering are smoothed. New per-car gain ratios never exceed the previous recording-layer ceiling. When a recording is audible, procedural body/exhaust retain 18% gain, harmonic/turbine 6%, and sub 35%; a failed or silent recording retains the full fallback. The recording gain ceiling stays at 0.42. Recording low-pass cutoffs stay below 3.3 kHz and the load shelf boost is at most 1.25 dB. Impact/Nitro ducking and the master compressor remain active. The [Web Audio buffer-loop model](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio/#AudioBufferSourceNode) is used directly; normal driving never creates per-frame sources.

Electric recordings have a lower gain ceiling, no exhaust shelf boost and smooth zero-at-rest gating. The Mercedes pass-by is similarly gated because its road/motion texture should not play at a standstill. A silent gated recording does not lower the procedural bed. EV pitch uses the existing single-gear motion, so adding the recording introduces no combustion shift drop.

## Resource and lifecycle bounds

- No context, playback or recording request before a user gesture unlock. No recording request for lobby music, muted/hidden/inactive state or an engine mixer at zero.
- One selected-bank request at a time; 8-second fetch/decode ceiling; at most 256 KiB encoded input. Car changes and inactivity abort stale requests. Late completion cannot create voices or retain a cancelled buffer.
- At most three active recorded sources. Bank changes stop and disconnect previous sources before replacement; related-car changes reuse the existing bank and voices.
- LRU: at most three decoded banks and 3 MiB PCM. A decode may temporarily hold one extra bank before eviction. Browser decoder overhead and garbage-collection timing are outside this accounting.
- Failed banks retain the original synthesis for the current audio instance without retry storms. Unknown future car IDs safely retain synthesis until explicitly mapped. A guard prevents a combustion recording from being assigned to an explicitly electric vehicle.
- Existing engine mixer, master compressor, mute/pause/hidden fades and 240 ms idle suspension apply. Disposal aborts requests, drops buffers, stops voices and disconnects the graph. Native timers retain their global receiver.

`createAudio().recordingStatus()` remains a read-only diagnostic: selected/active bank, voices, cache/decoded bytes, pending/fetches/failures and blend. It does not unlock or request audio. No main.js integration change is required.

## Listening-feedback mix revision — 3 October

The user listened to both upgraded-car samples and reported that they were still too harsh or synthetic. Numeric peak/resource passes had not established perceived quality. The revised mix reduces the procedural tones substantially under recordings, applies a softer finite-harmonic pulse and lower procedural filter, and finally includes the previously unducked turbine oscillator in recording attenuation. Recorded-loop pitch changes are narrower and slower, with less top-end and low-shelf emphasis. Recordings become more prominent relative to synthesis without increasing source gain.

Sound-only shift response now uses a 220 ms, 12% torque dip, slower pitch automation and smoother amplitude automation. This changes audio envelopes, not physics, shift thresholds, gear counts, user input or handling. Nitro's pitched core is one-quarter its former gain, its low sine weight is halved, and pressure air/release filters and envelopes are softer. A boost-local high-pass removes very low noise; the shared noise source and lobby/tyre/soundscape branches are unchanged. No oscillator, recording voice or noise buffer was added.

The expanded five-suite run passes **53/53 tests**. New checks cover upper harmonic power, per-frame shift torque continuity, actual recording-versus-failure graph gains across the two upgraded cars plus hybrid/EV, turbine attenuation, unchanged fallback availability, no new driving allocations, the existing 0.42 source-gain ceiling, narrower recording pitch, and pause behavior. Final browser renders passed for all 33 cars, and the user subsequently approved the two revised samples: “Yes, this sounds better.” The listening verdict comes from that explicit reply, not from numeric tests; broad physical-device/speaker coverage remains unverified.
