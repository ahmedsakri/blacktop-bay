Real-car model collection

These 16 car representations across 13 brands use individually credited artists’ geometry. We preserve the represented model identity and do not present a road model as a different racing variant. Each source is adapted for interactive browser use; handling, upgrades and Nitro are independently authored arcade mechanics.

Vortex, Vector & Zenith — Formula concept model

Vortex P1, Vortex X, Vector F and Zenith FX are adapted from F1 2026 concept (polygon model) by Qvist_designs, licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International.

We used the publicly redistributed model and component preparation from the APEX project by filan214. That adaptation grouped the surface into named assemblies, added illustrative seams, applied red and graphite materials, and removed uniform vertex colours.

For Blacktop Bay, the geometry was welded and simplified into two detail levels while retaining its sculpted shape and sixteen named parts. The game adjusts scale, orientation, materials and wheel pivots. Vector F adds original sprint aero details, while Zenith FX adds an original enclosed-canopy concept package. Handling and all vehicle names are fictional game design.

This is an independent concept model. No endorsement by Qvist_designs, the APEX project, any racing team, Formula One or the FIA is implied.

Apex, Torque, Kestrel, Corsair & Stratus — GT source

Apex GT, Apex Sprint, Torque R, Torque RS, Kestrel GT-R, Corsair GTS and Stratus GT3 are adapted from the Ferrari 458 Spider model by vicent091036, distributed through the official Three.js examples under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. The Three.js maintainer’s media-license audit records this license; the original Sketchfab listing is currently unavailable.

For Blacktop Bay, the mesh was decoded, welded and simplified into two detail levels, preserving named parts and surface normals. The game adjusts scale, orientation and materials, animates wheel assemblies, and adds original fixed racing roofs, roll cages, aero and liveries. Corsair GTS adds an open-cockpit cup package; Stratus GT3 adds long-tail endurance details. The vehicle names and handling are fictional.

The geometry is based on the credited Ferrari 458 Spider source. Blacktop Bay is not affiliated with or endorsed by Ferrari, the original creator, Three.js or any racing team.

Complete GT model attribution and modification notice · Full CC BY 4.0 license

Spectre & Cinder builds — original Prototype bodywork

Spectre LM and Spectre LM-R use an original closed-cockpit body profile by AppsOverFlow. Cinder R and Cinder RX use an original open-cockpit body profile by AppsOverFlow. These procedural body surfaces, cockpit details and racing aero are shaped in code; the two builds in each pair share a profile with their own proportions, livery and tuning.

These four Prototype builds reuse adapted wheel geometry from the Ferrari 458 Spider source by vicent091036, licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. The game scales and positions these wheel assemblies for each body and adjusts their materials and animation pivots. The original bodywork does not replace or remove the wheel-source attribution.

The twenty original builds combine licensed source geometry with original coachwork and racing adaptations. The vehicle names and setups are fictional; these are not twenty independently sourced models. No manufacturer or racing-team affiliation is implied.

Additional original coachwork and aero

Vela R adds an offset-cockpit sports-racer package, and Aurora LMX adds finned endurance bodywork. AppsOverFlow authors the additional coachwork and aero for these fictional builds.

Each build also reuses licensed GT parts from the Ferrari 458 Spider source by vicent091036 under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. Circuit outlines and arcade adaptations

The Grand Prix venue pack derives geographic outlines from f1-circuits by Tomislav Bacinger, copyright © 2019–2025, under the MIT License. The original data is unofficial. AppsOverFlow scales, smooths and widens the outlines for compact arcade racing, relocates starting grids where necessary and authors a non-crossing reinterpretation of Suzuka.

Official venue names, lengths and the calendar were checked against Formula 1’s 2026 calendar on 1 October 2026. The pack includes 23 current-calendar venues and Sakhir and Jeddah as original-calendar bonuses, alongside nine original Blacktop Bay courses. Playable lap lengths differ from the real circuits; elevation, banking and surveyed detail are not reproduced.

Blacktop Bay is an independent arcade game. These adaptations are not official Formula 1 assets, licensed circuit replicas or an endorsement by Formula One, the FIA or the venues.
