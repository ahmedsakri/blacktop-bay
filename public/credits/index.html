Vortex P1 & Vortex X — Formula concept model

The Vortex P1 and Vortex X builds are adapted from F1 2026 concept (polygon model) by Qvist_designs, licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International.

We used the publicly redistributed model and component preparation from the APEX project by filan214. That adaptation grouped the surface into named assemblies, added illustrative seams, applied red and graphite materials, and removed uniform vertex colours.

For Blacktop Bay, the geometry was welded and simplified into two detail levels while retaining its sculpted shape and sixteen named parts. The game adjusts scale, orientation, materials and wheel pivots. Handling and the Vortex vehicle names are fictional game design.

This is an independent concept model. No endorsement by Qvist_designs, the APEX project, any racing team, Formula One or the FIA is implied.

Complete model attribution and modification notice · Full CC BY 4.0 license

Apex & Torque builds — GT model source

Apex GT, Apex Sprint, Torque R and Torque RS are adapted from the Ferrari 458 Italia model by vicent091036, distributed through the official Three.js examples under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. The Three.js maintainer’s media-license audit records this license; the original Sketchfab listing is currently unavailable.

For Blacktop Bay, the mesh was decoded, welded and simplified into two detail levels, preserving named parts and surface normals. The game adjusts scale, orientation and materials, animates wheel assemblies, and adds original fixed racing roofs, roll cages, aero and liveries. The vehicle names and handling are fictional.

The geometry is based on the credited Ferrari 458 Italia source. Blacktop Bay is not affiliated with or endorsed by Ferrari, the original creator, Three.js or any racing team.

Complete GT model attribution and modification notice · Full CC BY 4.0 license

Spectre & Cinder builds — original Prototype bodywork

Spectre LM and Spectre LM-R use an original closed-cockpit body profile by AppsOverFlow. Cinder R and Cinder RX use an original open-cockpit body profile by AppsOverFlow. These procedural body surfaces, cockpit details and racing aero are shaped in code; the two builds in each pair share a profile with their own proportions, livery and tuning.

These four Prototype builds reuse adapted wheel geometry from the Ferrari 458 Italia source by vicent091036, licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. The game scales and positions these wheel assemblies for each body and adjusts their materials and animation pivots. The original bodywork does not replace or remove the wheel-source attribution.

The fourteen-build garage combines licensed source geometry with original coachwork and racing adaptations. The vehicle names and setups are fictional; these are not fourteen independently sourced models. No manufacturer or racing-team affiliation is implied.

GT wheel source and modification notice · Full CC BY 4.0 license

Kestrel, Mirage, Solstice & Tempest — additional coachwork

Kestrel GT-R uses a long-nose GT time-attack design; Mirage LMP has a low closed canopy; Solstice One is a single-seat open speedster; Tempest XR uses an extreme-aero prototype design. AppsOverFlow authors the additional coachwork and aero for these fictional builds.

Each build also reuses licensed GT parts from the Ferrari 458 Italia source by vicent091036 under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. Adaptations include changed proportions, positioning, materials, racing details and animation setup. The shared source attribution remains applicable alongside the newly authored geometry.
