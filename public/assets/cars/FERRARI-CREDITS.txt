The Three.js Draco edition was decoded into two browser-oriented detail levels.
The painted body, glazing, chrome and articulated wheels, rims, tyres and wheel
brakes retain their original visible geometry and surface normals. Only secondary
interior/detail surfaces are welded and meshoptimizer-simplified. This preserves
the source's smooth coachwork and recognizable wheel spokes; the earlier broad
permissive simplification distorted those reflective surfaces and was replaced.
The high-detail asset has 251,076 triangles (6,942,760 bytes); the mobile asset
has 209,180 triangles (5,727,276 bytes). Both retain 179,926 protected triangles,
named nodes and source materials. Runtime roofs, aero and liveries add geometry.
The files require no runtime decoder. The original model contained no image textures.

Reproduction: scripts/rebuild-gt-assets.mjs accepts the original licensed source
path and documents pinned offline tool dependencies. It does not fetch assets.

Secondary-detail simplification uses ratios 0.4 (high) / 0.17 (mobile), errors
0.003 / 0.008, and normal weights 0.05, while all protected surfaces remain intact.

The Apex GT, Apex Sprint, Torque R, Torque RS and Kestrel GT-R in-game builds further adjust scale, orientation and materials, animate
wheel assemblies, and add original fixed racing roofs, roll cages, aero and liveries. These game
variants, their names and handling are fictional. They are based on the
credited Ferrari 458 Italia mesh, not claimed to be wholly original models.
